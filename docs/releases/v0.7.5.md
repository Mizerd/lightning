# Lightning 0.7.5

Sixty-one commits since v0.7.4, and the largest visual change the project has
had. The whole design system was rebuilt, all eleven themes were rebuilt on top
of it, and then a theme **editor** landed so you can build your own. Alongside
that: ten languages, a portable mode, panels that resize and hide, folders in
the Spaces rail, profile and Space banners, and the first published **macOS**
build.

As always, what follows separates what was measured from what was not. Several
of this round's fixes came from screenshots and captures rather than from
reading code and guessing — and two hypotheses written down as fact were killed
by measurement.

## A new design system

The old palette had a structural fault that contrast checks could not see:
**collapsed elevation.** Several surface roles — the raised card, hover,
selected, the reaction pill — resolved to *the same literal*, dE 0.00 apart. The
interface had a surface ladder on paper and one flat plane on screen.

Every theme now has a real ladder, and the tokens behind it are semantic rather
than a pile of hex. On top of that:

- **A real type scale**, replacing a set of per-site font sizes that had drifted
  far enough apart that identical buttons were 30 px and 40 px tall side by side.
- **Honest status colours**, and chip ink held to AA against *the tint it
  actually sits on* — a chip paints its ink on a 14% wash of that same ink, so
  checking it against the page background was checking the wrong thing.
- **The message row and the reply quote rebuilt**, with the action bar in the
  row's corner rather than floating over the text.
- **Room list, rail and popovers made one family**, and the settings screen,
  dialogs and media chrome brought into it.
- **Identity colours that are actually distinct.** The old set spent two of its
  nine slots on collisions — closest pair dE 5.6, which is "the same colour" to
  a reader — while every one of them passed AA. Legibility was never the failing
  property.

## Eleven themes, and one of your own

All eleven were rebuilt against the new ladder. One finding is worth stating
because it is not obvious: **an accent cannot double as a link ink.** White text
on the accent is pinned at 3:1, which caps the accent's luminance, while an AA
link needs more than that cap on the raised surfaces. Over-determined. Eight
themes had routed `link` to `accent` and their links were failing at 2.4–3.8:1
by construction. Every theme now owns a link literal.

Then the **theme editor**: click any part of a live sample window, or a role in
the list, and recolour it. Keep as many themes as you like, name them, and
**Share** one as a block of text anybody can paste back in with **Import**.

Two things about it are deliberate. The sample window is a *sample*, never one
of your rooms. And the editor's own chrome follows the theme you have selected
but is never painted from the palette you are **editing** — you can set the
panel and the body ink to the same colour in two clicks, and if the editor
followed that, the role list, the picker and the button that undoes it would all
vanish at the same moment.

Fallback room and user discs are now **derived from the theme** too. A deep
indigo window used to show amber and rust initials, because the disc palette was
one fixed ladder deliberately weighted warm. They are now nine hues in an arc
around the theme's own anchor — with one exception that names the rule: Storm's
shell is navy at 233° and its accent is the brand bolt at 46°, nearly opposite,
so **the accent anchors the discs unless it is nowhere near the surface they sit
on, in which case the surface wins.**

## Ten languages

English, Arabic, Bengali, Chinese (Simplified), French, Hindi, Indonesian,
Portuguese, Russian and Spanish — 1,922 strings each, switchable without a
restart, right-to-left included.

Plurals are the part that would have been embarrassing to get wrong. English
needs its own catalogue purely so `%n room(s)` does not ship its literal "(s)";
Arabic has six plural forms and its zero form is *"لا أعضاء"* — "no members" —
which drops `%n` entirely. So a plural form may drop `%n`, and **only** `%n`,
and only a plural form: every other placeholder must survive in every form, or a
missing `%1` silently loses the room name.

## macOS, published for the first time

There is now a macOS download. Read this before taking it:

- **Apple Silicon only.** No Intel build exists.
- **macOS 26 or newer.** The floor is derived from the Qt frameworks the app
  links, not chosen; on macOS 15 or earlier it will not launch.
- **Unsigned and un-notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks it until you allow it
  once: open it, let macOS refuse, then System Settings → Privacy & Security →
  **Open Anyway**. The README has the click-by-click version.
- **It does not update itself.** Lightning will tell you a new version exists;
  installing it means downloading the next zip. The in-app installer has no
  macOS path and does not pretend to.
- **Nobody has clicked through it on a Mac.** The pipeline proves the bundle's
  frameworks load and the binary runs. Everything else on macOS is **NOT
  TESTED**. Reports welcome.

The release never depends on the Mac: the build job is non-blocking, so that
host being offline publishes a release without a macOS asset rather than failing
the eight other packages.

## Portable mode

`--portable`, or a `lightning-portable` marker beside the executable, keeps
**everything** Lightning writes inside its own folder — settings, caches, the
encryption store and the saved Matrix session, which now travels in the folder
rather than the OS credential vault.

One fix here mattered more than the feature: an in-app update was **deleting the
portable folder's session and keys**, because the updater replaced the directory
that the state lived inside.

## Space, room and people work

- **Profile banners** (MSC4427 over MSC4133), read and written under **both** the
  standard field name and the one Commet already ships, so a banner set in
  Lightning shows up there and the other way round.
- **Space banners** — a real image on a Space's front page, set by whoever the
  Space's own power levels allow. Matrix specifies no room banner, so this is
  Lightning's own state event in its own namespace; clients that do not know it
  simply show none.
- **Rail folders and drag ordering.** Group Spaces into folders; a collapsed
  folder still carries the unread count of everything inside it, so filing
  something away never hides it.
- **Every panel resizes and hides** — the room list, the right-hand panel, and
  the Spaces rail. Widths persist per account.
- **Close to the system tray** instead of quitting, with an unread count in the
  tooltip.
- **Room favourites**, pinned in their own section like Element classic, with a
  divider that no longer disappears when the row above or below it is selected.
- Change your **own display name**, and see profile changes phrased properly in
  the timeline instead of as raw state.
- Fenced code renders in a **real code block** instead of body text.

## Fixes from the tester rounds

- **The room list stopped bouncing.** Clicking an older room moved it up and
  then dropped it back. The cause was that a room's ordering timestamp came from
  its latest event *including state events*, which appear when a room's timeline
  loads and vanish when it unloads. Fixed at the source — the stamp is now the
  latest **message** — and structurally, since the field is now monotonic.
- **Images and videos show a preview before you send them**, including one
  pasted from the clipboard, and a reply to an image shows its thumbnail both in
  the quote and while you type.
- **Right-click an image** for copy, save, forward, reply and open, in the
  timeline and full screen. **Double-click anyone** to open their profile.
- **A run of deleted messages collapses into one line.**
- **You appeared offline to yourself.** Presence is polled for the users on
  screen and your own row was never in that set.
- **The GIF settings looked like they reset every launch.** They never did —
  the combo box was reporting index 0 because `indexOfValue()` returns -1 at
  creation time and a `Math.max(0, …)` was masking it. The setting was correct
  all along; the control was lying about it.
- **Cancel a stuck send**, and a real progress bar while media uploads.
- **The scroll teleport**, named by a capture rather than guessed at: four
  separate paths could move a reader, and the reported one was not the obvious
  one. Middle-click autoscroll and keyboard paging now own the scroll the same
  way the wheel does.
- The theme editor's name field **saves as you type** rather than only on Enter.
- The member profile card **scales with the window**, instead of being a 296 px
  postage stamp on a 4K display.

## A measurement instead of a fix

A tester reported having to press the quote key twice to type `¨`. The obvious
suspect was the composer's mention highlighter disturbing the input method.
Rather than ship a fourth speculative fix, this round sent a real
`QInputMethodEvent` preedit into the production composer — plain, and again with
a live mention in the field so the highlighter was actively formatting the same
document. **The preedit survives and composes in one further keystroke**, and a
negative control in the same test proves the assertion is not vacuous. So the
double press is the keyboard layout's own dead-key behaviour, and Lightning
drops nothing. The test stays, because three per-keystroke write-backs live in
that composer and all three must keep guarding themselves.

## Not tested

Live validation not performed for this release: the macOS build beyond
`--version` and `--build-info`; profile and Space banner round trips against a
homeserver, and their permission gates; rail folder persistence across a
restart; tray behaviour on Windows; and Element interoperability of anything in
this round. Voice and video calls remain unavailable — the signalling and media
stack exist in the codebase but stay disabled, because no answered call has been
validated on a real network.

Windows and macOS packages remain **unsigned**. The signed update manifest is
the integrity guarantee on every platform.
