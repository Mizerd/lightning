# Lightning 0.9.0

The largest feature round this client has had. The last few releases went into
calls; this one goes into everything around them — finding things, sending
things, moderating things, and deciding what your own client tells other
people about you.

Ten features. The two worth the version number are that **custom emoji work
inside a message** and that **you can sign your other devices in from this one
with a code**, but the one you will probably use most often is the media
browser.

## Finding things you have already sent

**Media, files and links, over a room's whole history.** The old Media tab
showed whatever the timeline happened to have loaded, so finding an image from
March meant scrolling the conversation back to March. This walks the room's
history on its own, in the background, and tells you how far it has got.

That last part matters more than it sounds. "No images" after sixty messages
and "no images in twelve thousand messages, all of history" are different
answers, and a browser that draws both as an empty grid is lying about the
second. The strip under the toolbar always says how much has been read, and
whether anything was undecryptable — history that exists and cannot be read is
a third state, and in an encrypted room it would otherwise just look like less
media.

Search by filename, caption, sender or link; filter by who sent it; grid for
pictures and a dated list for everything else.

## Custom emoji, inside a message

MSC2545 image packs could already be browsed and sent as stickers. Now their
`emoticon` images work the way people actually want them to: type `:blob` and
the installed packs offer their matches, and the emoji lands inline in the
sentence rather than as a separate picture.

They arrive from other clients too, which took more work than sending them:
matrix-sdk-ui sanitises incoming HTML with a hard-coded configuration that
strips the very attribute that marks an emoji, so Lightning reads the raw
event instead. The image is rebuilt from validated parts before it renders —
only an `mxc://` source is accepted, and the size is fixed here rather than
taken from the sender, who would otherwise be choosing how much of your
message their picture covers.

**Packs are editable now.** Rename a shortcode you typed wrong, remove an
image, rename a pack, or empty one. Room packs need the room's permission,
exactly as adding to one does.

## Signing in another device with a code

Point your phone at Lightning, or paste the code your phone is showing, and
the new device is signed in **and verified** — your cross-signing and backup
keys travel over the encrypted channel, so it can read your existing
conversations immediately rather than needing a separate verification round
afterwards. This is MSC4108, the same mechanism Element uses.

Two digits appear on both devices partway through. They are not a formality:
they prove the two devices are talking to each other and not to something in
between. If they do not match, stop.

Lightning does the already-signed-in half of this — showing a code, or taking
the text of one. Signing *Lightning itself* in from a code is not implemented,
deliberately: it would mean asking your homeserver for an OAuth capability
this client currently, on purpose, does not request.

## Moderation lists

Mjolnir-style policy lists: read the ban rules a room publishes, publish and
remove your own where the room's power level allows, and follow lists other
people maintain.

**Following a list does not block anyone by itself.** Lightning tells you when
someone is covered by a list you follow, and you decide. A published list is
somebody else's judgement, and quietly acting on it — with no way to see that
it happened, or why — is a different thing from acting on it.

Legacy lists are readable: Mjolnir shipped these events before the spec
settled, and the rooms people actually follow still carry the old spellings.

## Places people send you

Locations shared from a phone — Element X and Element mobile both do this,
including live shares — used to arrive here as "unsupported" with a line of
text. They now render as a card: the place, the sender's stated accuracy, an
"Open in a map" link, and for a live share whether it is still current rather
than an hour-old position shown as if it were now.

Lightning does not *send* locations, and that is a decision rather than a gap.
From a desktop, sharing a place is pasting a map link, which an ordinary
message already is; and a machine with no idea where it is has no business
publishing a "live" position. There is no map inside Lightning either — map
tiles would mean every reader's computer contacting a tile server the moment
a message renders — so the card links out instead.

## What your client tells other people

A new **Privacy & security → Reading and typing** section.

**Read receipts** can be public (as before), private, or off. Private uses the
Matrix `m.read.private` receipt: the server records it, so your own other
devices still clear their unread badge, and nobody else ever sees it. Off
tells nobody at all, including your own devices — that is the honest cost of
it. Either way, your own place in a conversation is still saved, and receipts
you have already sent cannot be taken back.

**Typing notices** can be switched off. They are the highest-frequency thing a
chat client discloses — every few keystrokes, to everyone in the room — and
they say when you are at the keyboard, not just what you eventually send.
Switching them off mid-sentence stops the current one immediately rather than
waiting for the server to time it out.

**Notification previews can differ for encrypted rooms.** A notification body
is handed to the desktop's notification daemon in plain text, where it may
reach a log or a lock screen — outside everything the room's encryption
guarantees. Wanting full previews from a project room and nothing but "New
message" from an encrypted one is an ordinary wish that one setting could not
express.

**Optionally refuse insecure devices** (MSC4153): do not share room keys with,
or decrypt messages from, devices their owner has not verified. It is off by
default, because turning it on makes anyone who has not verified their own
devices unreadable to you and you to them, and it applies at the next start —
the SDK reads it when it builds a client and offers no way to change it while
one is running.

## Around the edges

**Forward several messages to several rooms at once**, with or without naming
where they came from — and be told which copies failed, rather than a single
"sent" that was true for one of twelve.

**A display name and avatar for one room only.** Useful when the name you use
among friends is not the one you want in a work Space.

**Reply and mark-as-read straight from a notification**, where the desktop
supports it. A notification card outlives the account that raised it, so
Lightning refuses to act on one after you have switched accounts rather than
sending a reply from the wrong identity — which would succeed, and which
nothing would tell you about.

**Mute and deafen from anywhere in the window** (Ctrl+Shift+U and
Ctrl+Shift+H), and a **floating always-on-top call window** when Lightning is
minimised.

## From the first day of live testing

Everything below was reported by the maintainer using the round above on a
real account, and fixed before the release:

- **Widgets can be added and removed** from the room's Widgets tab when your
  power level allows it — a web page, an Etherpad pad, a Jitsi meeting, a
  video, an image, a Grafana dashboard. Still opened in your browser, never
  embedded.
- **The room information tabs wrap** onto two rows in a narrow panel instead
  of pushing the panel off the window.
- **Settings opens instantly** after the first time — the screen is built
  once and kept, rather than rebuilt on every open (that was the
  half-second). Its header is the same height as the room header, so the top
  of the window no longer jumps when you open it.
- **Light themes have no pure-white patches** — the search field, composer
  bar and status strip now carry a whisper of the theme's own hue.
- **Changing the GIF provider re-runs your search** instead of showing
  trending until you type again.
- **The "no key backup" notice on Home** now says what to do and opens the
  Sessions section, where backup is set up.
- **Read receipts no longer vanish during a call.** Readers whose marker
  sits on a call-membership update (which every participant re-publishes
  while a call runs) are shown on the message or call card above it.
- **The encryption lock sits next to the room name** instead of at the far
  end of the header.
- **The call popout shows everyone** — a tile per participant, and each
  screen share in a box of its own.
- **A participant's volume set before their audio arrives now applies when
  it does**, which is the likely cause of "volume 0 doesn't mute" reported
  from Windows. Not yet confirmed on Windows.
- **The GIF button's tooltip no longer sits under the picker**, and the
  send-options menu opens above the composer instead of over it.
- Sending a location was removed (paste a map link instead); received
  locations still show with an "Open in maps" link.

## Known limitations

* **What was used for real, and what was not.** Exercised against a real
  homeserver: the media browser, custom emoji, forwarding, per-room profiles,
  a notification reply sent from a real KDE notification, widget add and
  remove, the wrapped room-information tabs, Settings opening without a
  GUI stall, the light-theme tint and the GIF provider switch. Covered by
  automated tests only, never used against a real server, another device or
  another client: the call keys, the floating call window's new grid,
  sign-in-by-code beyond its error paths, MSC4153, policy lists, received
  locations, receipts on call cards and membership updates, the lock beside
  the room name, and the late-applied participant volume (the likely cause
  of the Windows "volume 0 does not mute" report, unconfirmed on Windows).
* **Sign-in by code needs a homeserver that supports the MSC4108 rendezvous
  endpoint.** Lightning reports when it does not, but this has not been tried
  against one that does.
* **Locations can be read but not sent**, as above.
* **Switching themes still blocks the window for about a second**; measured,
  not addressed in this release.
* **macOS remains untested** for everything in this release, as in the last.
